\problem{P24}{Total Remaining Distance}{A distributional recursion}
Starting at $R_0=x\in[0,1)$, choose $R_j$ uniformly from $(R_{j-1},1)$ using fresh independent randomness. Define
\[
Q=\sum_{j\ge1}(1-R_j).
\]
Prove that $Q$ is almost surely finite, find its mean and variance, and obtain the Laplace transform $L(t)=\E e^{-tD}$ of $D=Q/(1-x)$ for $t\ge0$.
\solution For independent uniform variables $U_j$, the remaining distances have the representation
\[
1-R_j=(1-x)\prod_{i=1}^jU_i.
\]
Nonnegativity permits summation under expectation:
\[
\E Q=(1-x)\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j}=1-x.
\]
Finite expectation of a nonnegative variable implies almost sure finiteness.

The normalized sum satisfies $D=U(1+D')$, where $D'$ has the law of $D$ and is independent of $U$. Its second moment is finite: the $L^2$ norm of every partial sum is bounded by $\sum_{j\ge1}3^{-j/2}<\infty$, and monotone convergence then applies. Thus
\[
\E D=1,\qquad
\E D^2=\frac13(1+2\E D+\E D^2)=\frac32.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{\E Q=1-x,\qquad \Var Q=\frac{(1-x)^2}{2}.}
\]
For $t>0$, the distributional recursion yields
\[
L(t)=\int_0^1e^{-tu}L(tu)\dd u,
\qquad tL(t)=\int_0^te^{-s}L(s)\dd s.
\]
The right side is differentiable and $L(t)>0$, so
\[
\frac{L'(t)}{L(t)}=\frac{e^{-t}-1}{t}.
\]
Integration with $L(0)=1$ gives
\[
\boxed{L(t)=\exp\!\left\{\int_0^t\frac{e^{-s}-1}{s}\dd s\right\}.}
\]
The integrand extends continuously to $-1$ at zero, so the expression also covers $t=0$.
\insight Although individual gaps shrink multiplicatively, their total satisfies a simple recursive sum. Establish finiteness of moments before manipulating the moment equations.
