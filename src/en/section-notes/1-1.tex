Conditional and unconditional independence must be distinguished. A latent coin type, a common failure, or selection can alter the joint observation law. Write likelihoods for the information actually recorded, then normalize using total probability and Bayes' formula. When an action is required, compare conditional expected payoffs with the stated losses included.

Symmetry can simplify probabilities and motivate dependent samples that reduce estimation variance, but any atom at zero must be retained. Under stopped sampling, use the stopping-count distribution to check unbiasedness. For jointly Gaussian variables, an orthogonal change of coordinates can isolate the direction affected by selection; correlations after selection must then be computed from the conditional moments.
