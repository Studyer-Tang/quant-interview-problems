Specify whether stopping includes the triggering observation and whether inspection occurs after each step or each round. For a nonnegative integer-valued stopping count $T$, tails give
\[
\E T=\sum_{n\ge0}\Prob(T>n),
\]
allowing infinite values. Adjacent tail differences give the probability mass function.

Negative logarithms of independent uniform factors are exponential increments, turning multiplicative shrinkage into an arrival-time problem. For comparisons between successive observations, equally likely ranks of independent identically distributed continuous samples may be simpler than densities. Positive-increment crossing problems can be analyzed by summing the densities of successive partial sums.

Before manipulating first- or second-moment recursions, establish finiteness of the relevant moments. For limits of infinite random sums or products, justify exchanging limits and expectations.
