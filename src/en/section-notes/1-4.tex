Ratios, extremes, and relative positions often call for a change of variables in a joint density. State the support and Jacobian explicitly. Conditional rescaling may remove the conditioning location and prove independence. Uniform cut points produce a constant density on the spacing simplex, but the spacings are dependent.

Geometric probability requires specified position and direction laws. A distance threshold can become a binomial neighborhood count, while intersection and pairing counts admit indicator decompositions. Concurrence and boundary cases need separate attention. Symmetry alone does not ensure that a mean exists.

For random colorings, an expected nonnegative integer count of bad events below one proves existence. For secrecy, compare ciphertext distributions conditional on messages; uniform ciphertext marginals alone do not establish joint secrecy.
